## First, the truth about "HMRC data"

You asked me to pull revenue from HMRC. Here's the honest position so you know exactly what the numbers below are and aren't:

- **HMRC does not publish revenue for named individual businesses.** Its data is anonymised and aggregated — you can see *5.63 million self-employment sources declared £118 billion of profit in 2022–23* (an average of ~£21k profit each, but heavily skewed by a few high earners), and which sectors earn most. You cannot see what any single shop earned. ([HMRC/ONS Personal Incomes Statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/personal-incomes-statistics-for-the-tax-year-2022-to-2023/personal-incomes-statistics-2022-to-2023-commentary))
- **Companies House** *does* hold public accounts for UK **limited companies** — but nearly every side hustle below is run by a **sole trader**, who files nothing public. The ones that are Ltd usually file "micro-entity" accounts that legally omit turnover.
- **So the best hard data for named side-hustle businesses comes from three places:** founders who publicly disclose their numbers (Starter Story, CNBC, DollarSprout, press interviews), **public platform sales counts** (Etsy shows lifetime sales on every shop — you can estimate revenue yourself), and **open-revenue dashboards** (Gumroad, Stripe). That's what I've used. Every figure below has a name and a source.

## The three worth studying closely (best working-mum fit)

### ⭐ Emily Odio-Sutton — the blueprint for *your* situation
This is the one to study, because her constraints match yours almost exactly.

- **Who:** A university administrator with a **full-time job and two young kids**. Started her Etsy shop as a **New Year's resolution in January 2023** after finding print-on-demand in a YouTube video — chose it precisely because it needed *no inventory, no shipping, low risk*.
- **What she sells:** Print-on-demand gifts — mugs, apparel, etc. — where a supplier (Printify/Printful) prints and ships each item only after a customer buys. She never touches stock.
- **The numbers:** **$560k revenue across her shops in two years, ~$23k/month, roughly 30% profit** (so ~$7k/month profit) — earned *around* the day job and kids. 94% of it from POD.
- **The one move that worked:** She read her reviews, noticed buyers kept saying "this is a gift," and **committed to listing two products a day targeting gift items for specific professions** (nurse gifts, teacher gifts, etc.). Volume + gift-niche SEO was the engine.
- **Replicate it:** Etsy shop + a POD supplier (Printify free tier) + Canva. Real cost is pennies per listing and your design time. The discipline is the product: *a set number of listings per day, aimed at a narrow gift niche.*
- **Honest catch:** 30% margin, not 90% — POD suppliers take the biggest cut, and Etsy ads eat into it. It's volume-driven. But it's genuinely doable around school hours.

### ⭐ Emily McDermott (Pretty Arrow) — the highest return on effort
- **What:** Budgeting **spreadsheet templates** for young women into personal finance. A digital file, made once, sold ~29,000 times.
- **Numbers:** **$125k/year from a $10 startup.** 100%-ish margin (no cost per sale). Grew it with email marketing and Facebook ads.
- **Why it matters for you:** Spreadsheets/planners are pure digital — no POD supplier taking a cut, no shipping. If you're comfortable in Excel/Google Sheets, this is the highest-margin model on the board. The catch is you need a *specific* audience and keyword niche (she picked "young women + budgeting"), not generic templates.

### ⭐ Jess Morton 🇬🇧 — proof it works here, for a mum, right now
- **Who:** A UK mum who **quit a "demanding" £43k teaching job** to resell vintage clothing. (Press-dated **this month**, July 2026 — the freshest case I found.)
- **Numbers:** **£118k revenue in ~2 years, averaging £5–6k/month, peaking around £15k/month**, across Vinted and eBay.
- **Replicate it:** Source underpriced vintage/branded pieces (car boots, charity shops, bulk lots), photograph well, list on Vinted (no seller fees) + eBay. Start with your own wardrobe to learn the flow.
- **Honest catch:** This is the one that *doesn't* go passive — it's a flexible job. Income tracks the hours you put into sourcing, photographing and posting. She reached £15k/month by treating it as full-time, not a side hustle. At side-hustle hours, think £1–2k/month (see Lynne Matthews, ~£2k/mo).

## The reality check you won't get from the "£10k/month" blogs

**The businesses above are top-decile outliers, not typical results.** The honest baseline:

- **The median Etsy seller earns ~$574/month in revenue** (not profit) — and most take months to reach even that. ([Etsy income data](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/average-etsy-seller-income))
- Emily Odio-Sutton spent time learning before traction; Ralu Petrea sold **2 copies on day one**; most named founders took **12–24 months** to reach the headline numbers.
- The UK self-employment average profit is ~£21k/year — and that's skewed upward by high earners. The typical side hustle makes hundreds a month, not thousands.

**What actually separates the winners from the median**, visible across every case above:
1. **A narrow, specific niche** — "budgeting for young women," "gifts for nurses," "college sports" — never "templates" or "clothes."
2. **Volume + consistency** — two listings a day, every day, for months.
3. **SEO discipline** — they rank for what buyers already search; they don't rely on going viral.
4. **12–24 months of patience** before the compounding kicks in.
